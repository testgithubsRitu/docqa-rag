Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is a technique that combines a search step with a language model.
Instead of relying only on what the model memorised during training, a RAG system first retrieves the
passages most relevant to the user's question from a document collection, and then gives those passages
to the model as context for its answer.

A typical RAG pipeline has four stages. First, documents are split into small overlapping chunks so that
each piece fits comfortably into a model's context window. Second, every chunk is converted into an
embedding, a vector of numbers that captures its meaning. Third, the embeddings are stored in a vector
database such as FAISS or ChromaDB, which can quickly find the vectors closest to a query vector.
Fourth, at question time the query is embedded in the same way, the nearest chunks are retrieved, and
they are placed in a prompt for the language model.

Tokenization is the step that turns raw text into tokens, the sub-word units a Transformer actually reads.
Embedding models such as MiniLM use a tokenizer first and then a Transformer encoder, whose self-attention
layers let every token look at every other token, to produce one vector for the whole sentence.

RAG helps reduce hallucination because answers can be traced back to source passages. Its quality depends
heavily on chunk size, chunk overlap and the quality of the embedding model.
